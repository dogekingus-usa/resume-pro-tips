How to Explain Career Gaps in Your Resume (With Examples)

Published: May 14, 2026 | Reading time: 3 min

Explaining career gaps on your resume can sometimes feel like walking a tightrope. However, with the right approach, you can turn these periods into positive narratives that reflect personal growth and development. This guide will walk you through how to effectively explain career gaps in your resume, ensuring they don't detract from your professional image.

Understand Why Career Gaps Matter

Career gaps are a common concern for job seekers. According to a survey by Glassdoor, 56% of hiring managers say that a gap on a resume will make them wonder if an applicant is qualified or reliable. But remember, these gaps aren't necessarily negative; they can be opportunities to highlight personal achievements and growth.

Be Honest but Strategic

The key to explaining career gaps lies in being honest while also strategically framing the situation positively. Here's how you can do it:

  • Avoid hiding or lying about your employment history. Honesty is crucial, as employers may find out eventually and that could damage your credibility.
  • Focus on the positive outcomes of your time off or break. Did you take a sabbatical to travel? Volunteer for a cause you're passionate about? Did you go back to school for a certification program?

Use Action Verbs and Quantify Results

To effectively explain your career gaps, use action verbs that demonstrate what you did during this time. For example:

  • "Travelled and volunteered in Southeast Asia for six months", or
  • "Completed a 10-month course in digital marketing with a 95% pass rate".

Provide Context and Relevance

When discussing your career gaps, provide context that shows relevance to the job you're applying for. Here's an example:

  • "Volunteered at a non-profit organization for six months during my sabbatical year. The experience enhanced my leadership and teamwork skills, which are directly applicable to this role."

Avoid Overexplaining

While it's important to be clear about your career gaps, avoid spending too much space on them. Keep your explanation concise and relevant. A brief mention is usually enough.

By following these tips, you can effectively explain any career gaps in a way that showcases your strengths and ensures your resume remains strong and compelling. Remember, your focus should always be on the positive outcomes of whatever time off or break you had.
