Should You Include Volunteer Experience on Your Resume? When and How

Volunteer experience can be a valuable addition to your resume, especially if you are seeking opportunities in fields that align with the skills gained during your volunteer work. Including volunteer roles can demonstrate commitment, passion, and real-world experience--qualities that employers value highly.

When Does Volunteer Experience Help?

Volunteer work is particularly beneficial for several situations:

  • You are in the early stages of your career: Volunteering provides a way to gain relevant experience, especially if you lack industry-specific employment history.
  • Your target industry values community service: Non-profit organizations and public sector jobs often highly value candidates with strong volunteer backgrounds.
  • You are making a career transition: If your current field is different from the one you want to enter, volunteering can help bridge the gap by showcasing transferrable skills.

How to Format Volunteer Experience on Your Resume

The way you present volunteer experience should be similar to how you format employment history. Here's a suggested structure:

  • Position Title and Organization Name: List the role, followed by the name of the organization (e.g., Volunteer Coordinator at XYZ Community Center).
  • Date Range: Include the start and end dates in parentheses. Use the present tense if your volunteer experience is ongoing.
  • Description of Responsibilities: Highlight key responsibilities and achievements, using bullet points to make it easy for the reader to scan.

Example Format

Volunteer Coordinator
ABC Non-Profit Organization
June 2019 - Present

  • Organized and led community outreach programs, increasing participation by 25%.
  • Maintained volunteer records and tracked progress on project milestones.
  • Developed marketing materials for fundraising events, raising $50,000 in three months.

Skills Gained Through Volunteer Work

Volunteer roles can offer a wide range of skills that are highly sought after by employers:

  • Leadership and Management: Organizing events, managing volunteers, and overseeing projects.
  • Communication and Interpersonal Skills: Working with diverse groups, active listening, and public speaking.
  • Project Management: Planning, organizing, and delivering successful initiatives.
  • Creativity and Problem Solving: Developing innovative solutions to community issues.
  • Teamwork and Collaboration: Working with a diverse team of volunteers or professionals.

Leadership Examples from Volunteer Experience

Highlighting specific leadership experiences can make your resume stand out. For example, if you led a volunteer group that successfully organized an event, detail the steps taken and the outcome:

Event Organizer, Community Outreach Program (May 2018 - April 2019)
  • Developed and executed a comprehensive marketing plan, resulting in a 30% increase in event attendance.
  • Managed a team of 15 volunteers to ensure smooth operation during the event, leading to high satisfaction ratings from attendees.

A Gap Filler Strategy for Your Resume

If you have experienced a career gap or are returning to work after a long absence, incorporating volunteer experience can serve as a bridge. Here's how:

  • Highlighting Continuous Learning: Detail any courses or certifications completed during your break that could be relevant to the job.
  • Showcasing Network Expansion: List connections made through volunteer work, such as industry contacts or mentors who might provide references.
  • Demonstrating Engagement: Highlight any projects or initiatives you undertook during your gap that demonstrate ongoing involvement and interest in the field.

Conclusion: Making Your Volunteer Experience Count

Including volunteer work on your resume can provide a significant advantage by showcasing additional skills, experience, and passion. Tailoring your resume to highlight how these experiences align with your career goals will make a compelling case for why you are the right candidate. Remember, it's not just about adding bullet points; it's about telling a story that resonates with potential employers.

By strategically presenting volunteer work on your resume, you can effectively bridge any gaps in employment history and demonstrate to hiring managers that you have the skills, dedication, and real-world experience they are looking for. Happy job hunting!
