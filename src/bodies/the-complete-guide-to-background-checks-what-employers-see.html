The Complete Guide to Background Checks What Employers See

Published: May 15, 2026 | Reading time: 4 min

Understanding what employers see during background checks is crucial for job seekers to manage their online presence and ensure they present themselves in the best possible light. This guide provides a comprehensive overview of the information employers typically gather, along with tips on how to prepare and handle these checks effectively.

The Types of Background Checks Employers Perform

Employers use background checks to verify the truthfulness of the information provided in a job application or resume. The type of check can vary depending on the nature of the role, industry, and company policy. Here are some common types:

  • Criminal History: This checks for any criminal records that could impact an individual's suitability for certain jobs.
  • Credit Reports: Often used in financial institutions or roles involving handling money, credit reports can show financial responsibility and stability.
  • Educational Verification: Verifies the authenticity of degrees, certifications, and other educational qualifications claimed by job candidates.
  • Military Records: Essential for positions within military organizations or those requiring specific security clearances.
  • Reference Checks: Contacting past employers or colleagues to validate information about a candidate's performance, work ethic, and reliability.

What Information Employers Can See Online

The internet is an essential tool for background checks. Here are some common online activities that can be visible to employers:

  • Social Media Profiles: Employment decisions based on social media content have been a topic of legal scrutiny, but many companies still use this as part of their process. Content related to illegal activities, discrimination, or inappropriate behavior is particularly concerning.
  • Public Records and Databases: Employers can access public records such as criminal databases, property ownership, and more. This helps in verifying personal information provided by candidates.
  • Professional Networks: Sites like LinkedIn provide a glimpse into professional history, skills, and endorsements from colleagues and clients.

Tips for Handling Background Checks

To make the background check process smoother, consider these actionable tips:

  1. Be Honest and Transparent: Provide all requested documents such as transcripts, diplomas, or certificates. Honesty can prevent issues down the line.
  2. Review Your Social Media Profiles: Regularly check your social media accounts for inappropriate content. Consider making these profiles private if they contain sensitive information that could be seen by employers.
  3. Update Your Resume and LinkedIn Profile: Ensure all the information you provide is accurate and up-to-date. Inconsistencies can lead to further verification steps which may delay the hiring process.

The Legal Aspects of Background Checks

Employers must handle background checks responsibly, as there are legal implications involved:

  • Avoid Discrimination: Ensure that the background check is relevant to job responsibilities. Unlawful discrimination based on race, gender, or other protected statuses is prohibited.
  • Adhere to State and Federal Laws: Different states have specific laws regarding what can be legally checked. For instance, some states restrict access to certain types of criminal records.

For job seekers, it's crucial to stay informed about your rights under these regulations. If you feel that a background check has been mishandled or is discriminatory, consulting with an attorney can provide guidance on next steps.

Conclusion

Understanding the extent of what employers see during background checks allows job seekers to take proactive measures in managing their digital footprint and presenting themselves accurately. By being transparent, keeping personal information updated online, and adhering to legal guidelines, you can ensure a smoother hiring process.
